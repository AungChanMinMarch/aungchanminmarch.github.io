Definition: Continuous Function

Let $(X, \topologyTau)$ and $(Y, \TauStar)$ be topological spaces. A function $f$ from $X$ into $Y$ is continuous relative to $\topologyTau$ and $\TauStar$, or $\topologyTau$-$\TauStar$ continuous, or simply continuous, iff the inverse image $f^{-1}[H]$ of every $\TauStar$-open subset $H$ of $Y$ is a $\topologyTau$-open subset of X, that is, iff $$H \in \TauStar \text{ implies } f^{-1}[H] \in \topologyTau$$

Eg 1.1

Consider the following topologies on $X = \{a,b,c,d\}$ and $Y = \{x,y,z, w\}$ respectively:
$$\topologyTau = \{X, \emptyset, \{a\}, \{a,b\}, \{a,b,c\} \}$$ $$\topologyTau^* = \{ Y, \emptyset, \{x\}, \{y\}, \{x,y\}, \{y,z,w\} \}$$ Also consider the functions f:X->Y and g:X—>Y defined by the diagrams below:

Are $f$ and $g$ continuous?

Since $\topologyTau^* = \{ Y, \emptyset, \{x\}, \{y\}, \{x,y\}, \{y,z,w\} \}$,

\begin{align*} Y &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[Y] = \{a,b,c,d\} = X &\in \topologyTau \\ \emptyset &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\emptyset] = \emptyset &\in \topologyTau \\ \{x\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{x\}] = \emptyset &\in \topologyTau \\ \{y\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{y\}] = \{a\} &\in \topologyTau \\ \{x,y\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{x,y\}] = \{a\} &\in \topologyTau \\ \{y,z,w\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{y,z,w\}] = \{a,b,c,d\} = X &\in \topologyTau \\ \end{align*} $$ \text{Hence } H \in \topologyTau^* \text{ implies } f^{-1}[H] \in \topologyTau$$

Therefore $f$ is continuous.

\begin{align*} Y &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & g^{-1}[Y] = \{a,b,c,d\} = X &\in \topologyTau \\ \emptyset &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\emptyset] = \emptyset &\in \topologyTau \\ \{x\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{x\}] = \{a,b\} &\in \topologyTau \\ \{y\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{y\}] = \emptyset &\in \topologyTau \\ \{x,y\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{x,y\}] = \{a, b\} &\in \topologyTau \\ \{y,z,w\} &\in \topologyTau^* \implies & f^{-1}[\{y,z,w\}] = \{c,d\} &\notin \topologyTau \\ \end{align*}

Hence the function $g$ is not continuous since $\{y, z, w \} \in \topologyTau^*$ but $g^{-1}[\{y,z,w\}] = \{c,d\} \notin \topologyTau$.

Eg 1.2 + Ex 2

  1. Eg 1.2: Consider any discrete space $(X, \discrete)$ and any topological space $(Y,\topologyTau)$. Then prove that every function $f: X \to Y$ is $\discrete$-$\topologyTau$ continuous.
  2. Ex 2: Prove: Let $f:X \to Y$ be any function. If $(Y, \indiscrete)$ is an indiscrete space, then $f:(X,\topologyTau) \to (Y, \indiscrete)$ is continuous for any $\topologyTau$.

  1. Since $(X, \discrete)$ is a discrete space, every subset of $X$ is open.

    Clearly $f^{-1}[H]$ is a subset of $X$ for any open subset $H$ of $Y$.

    Hence $f^{-1}[H]$ is open for any open subset $H$ of $Y$.

    Therefore $f$ is continuous.

  2. Since $(Y, \indiscrete)$ is an indiscrete space, $Y$ and $\emptyset$ are the only open subsets of $Y$.

    $f^{-1}[Y] = X$ and $f^{-1}[\emptyset] = \emptyset$.

    $X$ and $\emptyset$ belong to any topology $\topologyTau$ on $X$.

    Hence $f$ is continuous for any $\topologyTau$.

Proposition 7.1(Eg 1.3)

A function $f: X \to Y$ is continuous iff the inverse of each member of a base $\base$ for $Y$ is an open subset of $X$.

Suppose $f$ is continuous.

Let $\base$ be a base for a topology $\TauStar$ on $Y$.

Then $\base$ is a class of open sets of $Y$, i.e. $\base \subset \TauStar$

Hence if $B \in \base$, $B$ is an open set of $Y$. Since $f$ is continuous, $f^{-1}[B]$ is open.

Therefore $f^{-1}[B]$ is open for every set $B$ in $\base$.

Now suppose the inverse of each member of a base $\base$ for $Y$ is an open subset of $X$.

Let $H$ be an open subset of $Y$.
Since $\base$ is a base for the topology on $Y$, $H$ is the union of members of base $\base$.
\begin{align} H&=\cup_{i} B_{i}, \text{ where } B_i \in \base \\ f^{-1}[H] &=f^{-1}\left[\cup_{i} B_{i}\right] =\cup_{i} f^{-1}\left[B_{i}\right] \end{align} Since $f^{-1}[B]$ is an open subset of $X$ for each member $B \in \base$, each $f^{-1}\left[B_{i}\right]$ is open.
Hence $f^{-1}[H]$ is the union of open sets and hence $f^{-1}[H]$ is open. Thus $f$ is continuous.

CONTINUOUS FUNCTIONS AND ARBITRARY CLOSENESS

Let $X$ be a topological space. A point $p \in X$ is said to be arbitrarily close to a set $A \subset X$ if either
  1. $p \in A$ or
  2. $p$ is an accumulation point of $A$
Thus $p$ is arbitrarily close to $A$ if $p \in A \cup A’ = \closure{A} = \interior{A} \cup \b{A}$
