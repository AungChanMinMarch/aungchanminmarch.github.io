Let $X$ be a first countable space. Then the following are equivalent:
  1. $X$ is Hausdorff.
  2. Every convergent sequence has a unique limit.

By {% aTag collections.all "5208_10_2_ex_10" %}, we have (i) implies (ii)

Hence we need only show that (ii) implies (i).

Suppose $X$ is not Hausdorff.

Then there exist $a, b \in X, a \ne b$ with the property that every open set containing a has a non-empty intersection with every open set containing b.

Now let $\{G_{n}\}$ and $\{H_{n}\}$ be nested local bases at $a$ and $b$ respectively.

Then $G_{n} \cap H_{n} \ne \emptyset$ for every $n \in \N$

Since $G_{1} \cap H_{1} \ne \emptyset$, take $a_1 \in G_{1} \cap H_{1}$.

Since $G_{2} \cap H_{2} \ne \emptyset$, take $a_2 \in G_{2} \cap H_{2}$.

By repeating the process, we get a sequence $\seq{a_n}$ such that $a_{1} \in (G_1 \cap H_1), a_{2} \in (G_2 \cap H_2) , \dots$

Clearly, $\seq{a_n}$ converges to both $a$ and $b$. Hence there exists a sequence whose limit is not unique. In other words, (ii) implies (i).
