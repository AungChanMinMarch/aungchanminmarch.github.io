Example 3.2: Let $X$ be a $T_3$-space. Then prove that $X$ is also a Hausdorff space, $T_2$-space.

Let $X$ be a $T_3$-space.

Then $X$ is both a regular space and $T_1$ space.

For let $a,b \in X$ with $a \ne b$.

Since $X$ is a $T_1$-space, $\{a\}$ is a closed set.

Since $a \ne b$, $b \notin \{a\}$.

Since $X$ is a regular space, there exist disjoint open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $\{a\} \subset G$ and $b \in H$.

Hence there exist open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $a \in G$, $b \in H$ and $G \cap H = \emptyset$. Therefore $X$ is Hausdroff space.
