A topological space $X$ is regular iff it satisfies the following axiom:

$[R]$ If $F$ is a closed subset of $X$ and $p \in X$ does not belong to $F$, then there exist disjoint open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $F \subset G$ and $p \in Н$.

A regular space need not be a $T_{1}$-space.

Example 3.1: Consider the topology ${{tau}} =\{ X, \emptyset , \{a\}, \{b,c\} \}$ on the set $X = \{a, b, c\}$. Prove that $(X, {{tau}})4 is regular but not $T_1$.
The closed subsets of $X$ are $\emptyset, X, \{b,c\}$ and $\{a\}$.

Observe that $(X, {{tau}})$ does satisfy $[R]$.

On the other hand, $(X, {{tau}})$ is not a $T_1$-space since $\{b \}$ is not closed.

A regular space $X$ which also satisfies the separation axiom $[T_1]$, i.e. a regular $T_1$-space, is called a $T_3$-space.
