A completely regular space $X$ is also regular.

Let $F$ be a closed subset of $X$ and suppose $p \in X$ does not belong to $F$.

Since $X$ is completely regular, there exists a continuous function $f:X \to [0, 1]$ such that $f(p) = 0$ and $f[F] = \{1\}$.

Since $\R$ is Hausdorff space and every subspace of a Hausdorff space is also Hausdorff, $[0,1]$ is Hausdorff.

Since a Hausdorff space is always a $T_{1}$-space, $[0,1]$ is a $T_{1}$-space.

By {% aTag collections.all "5208_10_1" "Theorem 10.1" %}, $\{0\}$ and $\{1\}$ are closed.

Since $[0,1]$ is Hausdorff, by $[T_2]$, there exists open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $\{0\} \subset G, \{1\} \subset H$ and $G \cap H = \emptyset$.

Since $f$ is continuous and $G$ and $H$ are disjoint open sets, $\inverse{f}{G}$ and $\inverse{f}{H}$ are disjoint open sets.

\begin{align} f(p) = 0 &\in G & &\text{ and } & f(F) = \{1\} &\subset H \\ p &\in \inverse{f}{G} & &\text{ and } & F &\subset \inverse{f}{H} \\ \end{align}

Hence there exists open sets $\inverse{f}{G}$ and $\inverse{f}{H}$ such that $p \in \inverse{f}{G}$ and $F \subset \inverse{f}{H}$. Therefore, $X$ is also regular.
