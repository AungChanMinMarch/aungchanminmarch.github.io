Show that the property of being a regular space is hereditary, i.e. every subspace of a regular space is regular.

Let $(X, {{tau}})$ be a regular space and let $(Y, {{tau}}_{y})$ be a subspace of $(X, {{tau}})$.

Suppose $F$ is a ${{tau}}_Y$-closed subset of Y$ and $p \inY$ does not belong to $F$.

Then $F = {{tau}}_Y$-closure of $F$.

Since $p \notin F$, $p$ does not belong to ${{tau}}_Y$-closure of $F$.

Since ${{tau}}_Y$-closure of $F = Y \cap \closure{F}$, p does not belong to $Y \cap \closure{F}$.

Since $p \in Y$ and p \notin (Y \cap \closure{F})$, we have $p \notin \closure{F}$.

Hence $\closure{F}$ is closed and $p \in X$ does not belong to $\closure{F}$.

Since $(X, {{tau}})$ is regular, there exists ${{tau}}$-open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $$\closure{F} \subset G, p \in H \text{ and } G \cap H = \emptyset$$

Since $G, H \in {{tau}}$, we have $(Y \cap G) \in {{tau}}_Y$ and $(Y \cap H) \in {{tau}}_Y$

Since $F \subset Y$ and $F \subset \closure{F} \subset G$, we have $F \subset (Y \cap H)$.

Since $p \in Y$ and $p \in H$, we have $p \in (Y \cap H)$.

$(Y \cap G) \cap (Y \cap H) = Y \cap (G \cap H) = Y \cap \emptyset$

Hence, there exists ${{tau}}_Y$-open sets $Y \cap G$ and $Y \cap H$ such that $$F \subset (Y \cap G), p \in (Y \cap H) \text{ and } (Y \cap G) \cap (Y \cap H) = \emptyset$$

In other words, $(Y, {{tau}}_Y)$ is also regular.
