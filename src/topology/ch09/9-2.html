A topological space $X$ is a Hausdorff space or $T_2$-space iff it satisfies the following axiom:

$[T_2]$ Each pair of distinct points $a, b \in X$ belong respectively to disjoint open sets.

In other words, there exist open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $A \in G, b \in H$ and $G \cap H = \emptyset$

Observe that a Hausdorff space is always a $T_1$-space.

Theorem 10.3(Example 2.1)

Every metric space is a Hausdorff space.

Let $X$ be a metric space.

Let $a$ and $b$ be distinct points of $X$, i.e. $a \in X, b \in X, a \neq b$

Then by $[M_4]$, $d(a,b) > 0$. Let $d(a,b) = \ep$.

Consider the open spheres $G=S(a,{{r}})$ centered at $a$ with radius ${{r}}$ and $H = S(b, {{r}})$, centered at $b$ with radius ${{r}}$.

We need to prove $G \cap H= \emptyset$.

Suppose that $G \cap H \neq \emptyset$.

Let $p \in G \cap H$.

Then $p \in G$ and $p \in H$.

Hence $d(p,a) < {{r}}$ and $d(p,b) < {{r}}$.

By the Triangle Inequality,

\begin{align} d(a,b) &\leq d(a, p) + d(p, b)\\ &< {{r}} + {{r}} \\ = \frac{2\ep}{3} \end{align}

$d(a,b) < \frac{2\ep}{3}$ contradicts that $d(a,b) = \ep$.

Therefore $G \cap H = \emptyset$.

Now we have $a \in G, b \in H$ and $G$ and $H$ are disjoint open spheres. Thus $X$ is a Hausdorff space.
