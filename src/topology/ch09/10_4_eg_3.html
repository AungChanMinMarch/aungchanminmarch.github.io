{{title}}: Let $X$ be a $T_4$-space. Then prove that $X$ is also a $T_3$-space.

Let $X$ be a $T_4$-space.

Then $X$ is both a normal space and $T_1$ space.

Let $F$ be a closed subset of $X$ and $p \notin F$.

Since $X$ is a $T_1$-space, $\{p\}$ is a closed set.

Since $p \notin F$, $\{p\} \cap F = \emptyset$.

Since $F$ and $\{p\}$ are closed subsets of a regular space $X$, there exist disjoint open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $F \subset G$ and $\{p\} \subset H$.

Hence there exist open sets $G$ and $H$ such that $F \subset G$, $p \in H$ and $G \cap H = \emptyset$. Therefore $X$ is regular $T_1$ space. In other words, $X$ is a $T_3$-space.
