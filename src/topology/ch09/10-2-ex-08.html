Show that the property of being a Hausdorff space is hereditary, i.e. every subspace of a Hausdorff space is also Hausdorff.

Let $(X, \topologyTau)$ be a Hausdorff space and let $(Y, \topologyTau_{Y})$ be a subspace of $(X, \topologyTau)$.

Furthermore, let $a, b \in Y \subset X$ with $a \ne b$.

Since $(X, \topologyTau)$ is Hausdorff, there exists $G, H \in \topologyTau$ such that $a \in G, b \in H$ and $G \cap H = \emptyset$.

Since $G, H \in \topologyTau$, by definition of a subspace, $(Y \cap G) \in \topologyTau_{Y}$ and $(Y \cap H) \in \topologyTau_{Y}$.

Furthermore, since $a \in Y$ and $a \in G$, we have $a \in Y \cap G$

Similarly, since $a \in Y$ and $a \in H$, we have $a \in Y \cap H$.

\begin{align} (Y \cap G) \cap (Y \cap H) &= Y \cap (G \cap H)\\ &= Y \cap \emptyset \\ &= \emptyset \end{align}

Hence there exists $(Y \cap G) \in \topologyTau_{Y}$ and $(Y \cap H) \in \topologyTau_{Y}$ such that $a \in (Y \cap G)$, $b \in (Y \cap H)$, and $(Y \cap G) \cap (Y \cap H) = \emptyset$. Therefore $(Y, \topologyTau_{Y})$ is also a Hausdorff space.
