--- title: "Solved Problems no. 12 (Theorem 10.6 proof)" parent: 5208_10_4 order: 112 H: \comp{\closure{G}} ---

Let $X$ be a topological space. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $X$ is normal.
  2. If $H$ is an open superset of a closed set $F$, then there exists an open set $G$ such that $F \subset G \subset \closure{G} \subset H$.

Let $X$ be a topological space.

Suppose $X$ is normal.

Let $F \subset H$, where $F$ is closed and $H$ is open.

Since $H$ is open, $\comp{H}$ is closed.

Since $F \subset H$, we have $F \cap \comp{H} = \emptyset$.

Since $F$ and $\comp{H}$ are disjoint closed sets of a normal space $X$, there exists disjoint open sets $G$ and $G^*$ such that $F \subset G$ and $\comp{H} \subset G^*$.

Since $G$ and $G^*$ are disjoint, we have $G \cap G^* = \emptyset$. Hence $G \subset \comp{G^*}$.

Since $\comp{H} \subset G^*$, $\comp{G^*} \subset H$.

Thus we have $F \subset G \subset \comp{G*} \subset H$.

Since $G^*$ is open, $\comp{G^*}$ is closed.

Since $G \subset \comp{G^*}$ and $\comp{G*}$ is closed, we have $G \subset \closure{G} \subset \comp{G^*}$.

So $F \subset G \subset \closure{G} \subset \comp{G*} \subset H$. Therefore there exists open set $G$ such that $F \subset G \subset \closure{G} \subset H$.

Conversely, suppose that for every closed set $F$ and open set $H$ containing $F$, there exists open set $G$ such that $F \subset G \subset \closure{G} \subset H$.

Let $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$ disjoint closed sets.

Since $F_{1} \cap F_{2} = \emptyset$, $F_1 \subset \comp{F_2}$

Since $F_{2}$ is closed, $\comp{F_2}$ is open.

Hence there exists an open set $G$ such that $F_1 \subset G \subset \closure{G} \subset \comp{F_{2}}$

Since $\closure{G} \subset \comp{F_{2}}$, we have $F_{2} \subset {{H}}$

Since $G \subset \closure{G}$, we have $G \cap {{H}} = \emptyset$.

Since $\closure{G}$ is closed, ${{H}}$ is open.

Hence there exist open sets $G$ and ${{H}}$ such that $F_1 \subset G$ and $F_{2} \subset {{H}}$.

Therefore $X$ is normal.
