Show that the property of being a $T_1$-space is hereditary, i.e. every subspace of a $T_{1}$ space is also a $T_1$-space.

Let $(X, \topologyTau)$ be $T_1$-space and let $(Y, \topologyTau_{Y})$ be a subspace of $(X, \topologyTau)$.

We need to show that every singleton subset $\{p\}$ of $Y$ is a {{tauy}}-closed set, or equivalently, that $Y \backslash \{p\}$ is {{tauy}}-open.

Let $p \in Y$. Since $Y \subset X$, we have $p \in X$.

\begin{align} \text{Since } p \in Y &\subset X, \\ Y \backslash \{p\} &= (Y \cap X) \backslash \{p\} \\ &= Y \cap (X \backslash \{p\}) \end{align}

Since $(X, \topologyTau)$ is a $T_{1}$ space, $\{p\}$ is a $\topologyTau$-closed set. So $X \backslash \{p\}$ is $\topologyTau$-open.

Hence by definition of subspace, $Y \cap (X \backslash \{p\})$ is a {{tauy}}-open set. Therefore $Y \backslash \{p\}$ is a {{tauy}}-open set.

Thus $\{p\}$ is {{tauy}}-closed and $(Y, \topologyTau_{Y})$ is also a $T_{1}$ space.
