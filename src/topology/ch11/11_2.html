--- title: "COMPACT SETS" parent: topology11 key: 5208_11_2 order: 2 layout: toc.njk --- The concept of compactness is no doubt motivated by the property of a closed and bounded interval as stated in the classical Heine-Borel Theorem. Namely,
A subset $A$ of a topological space $X$ is compact if every open cover of $A$ is reducible to a finite cover.
In other words, if $A$ is compact and $A \subset \cup_i G_i$ where the $G_{i}$ are open sets, then one can select a finite number of the open sets $G_{i_1},\cdots, G_{i_n}$ so that $A \subset G_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup G_{i_n}$.
