--- title: "Theorem 3.4" parent: 5212_3_3_uniqueness order: 0 ---
Suppose $\vec{f}$ and $\partialwrt[\vec{f}]{y_j}, (j = 1,..., n)$ are continuous on the "box" $$ B = \cbracket{(t,y) | \abs{t-t_0} \leq a, \abs{\vec{y} - \vec{\eta}} \leq b}$$ Then there exists at most one solution of (3.22) satisfying the initial condition (3.23).

Suppose that $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ are two solutions of (3.22), (3.23) which both exist on some common interval $J$ containing $t_0$.

Since every solution of (3.22), (3.23) also satisfies the integral equation (3.21), we have $$\Phi_1(t) =\vec\eta + \int_{t_0}^{t} \vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s)) ds $$ $$\Phi_2(t) =\vec\eta + \int_{t_0}^{t} \vec{f}(s, \Phi_2(s)) ds $$ for every $t \in J$. Subtracting these two equations, we obtain \begin{align} \Phi_1(t) - \Phi_2(t) &= \int_{t_0}^{t} \vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s)) ds - \int_{t_0}^{t} \vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s)) ds \\ &= \int_{t_0}^{t} (\vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s)) - \vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s))) ds \\ \end{align} Taking norms and using $\abs{\vec{f}(t,\vec{y}) - \vec{f}(t, \vec{z})} \leq K \abs{\vec{y} - \vec{z}}$, we have \begin{align} \abs{\Phi_1(t) - \Phi_2(t)} &= \abs{\int_{t_0}^{t} (\vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s)) - \vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s))) ds} \\ &\leq \int_{t_0}^{t} \abs{\vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s)) - \vec{f}(s, \Phi_1(s))} ds \\ &\leq \int_{t_0}^{t} K \abs{\Phi_2(s) - \Phi_1(s)} ds \\ \end{align} since $(s, \Phi_1(s)) \in B, (s, \Phi_2(s)) \in B$ and $f$ satisfies Lipschitz condition on $B$.

If $t \geq t_0$

$$ \abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)} \leq 0 + \int_{t_0}^{t} K \abs{\Phi_2(s) - \Phi_1(s)} ds $$ By Gronwall Inequality, $$ \abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)} \leq 0 \cdot \exp(\int_{t_0}^{t} K ds) = 0 $$

If $t \leq t_0$

$$ \abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)} \leq 0 + \int_{t}^{t_0} K \abs{\Phi_2(s) - \Phi_1(s)} ds $$ By Gronwall Inequality, $$ \abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)} \leq 0 \cdot \exp(\int_{t}^{t_0} K ds) = 0 $$ For both cases, we get $$ \abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)} \leq 0$$ Since $\abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)}$ is non-negative, \begin{align} \abs{\Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t)} &= 0 \text{ for all } t \in J \\ \Phi_2(t) - \Phi_1(t) &= 0 \text{ for all } t \in J \\ \Phi_2(t) &= \Phi_1(t) \text{ for all } t \in J \\ \end{align} Thus there cannot be two distinct solutions and there exists at most one solution. This proves uniqueness.
