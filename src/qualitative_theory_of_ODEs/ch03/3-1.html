3.1 Existence in the Scalar Case

We begin with the problem of proving the existence of the solution $\phi$ of the scalar differential equation $$y' = f(t,y) \tag{3.1}$$, where $f$ is continuous function on a domain $D$, satisfying the initial conditions $$ \phi (t_0) = y_0 \tag{3.2}$$ on some interval containing $t_{0}$ . This is called a local problem, since it is concerned only with existence of solutions near the initial point $(t_{0}, y_{0})$.

Suppose $f$ is continuous in $D$ and that $(t_0, y_0)$ is an arbitrary point of $D$. Observe that the initial value problem (3.1), (3.2) is equivalent to the problem of finding a continuous function $y(t)$, defined in some interval $I$ containing $t_{0}$, such that $y(t)$ satisfies the integral equation $$ y(t) = y_{0} + \int_{t_0}^{t} f(s, y(s)) ds \tag{3.3}$$
