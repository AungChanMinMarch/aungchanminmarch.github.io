Construct the successive approximations to the solution $\phi$ of the differential equation $y' = y$ that satisfies $\phi(0)=1$
$$y'=y, \phi(0) = 1$$

We have $y'=f(t,y)=y$

The initial condition is $\phi(0)=1$. Hence $t_0 = 0, y_0 =1$

The successive approximation is

$$\phi_{j + 1}(t) = y 0 + \int_{t_0}^t f(s, \phi_j (s))ds, j=0, 1, 2, \cdots$$ $$\phi_0(t) = y_0 =1$$ \begin{align} \phi_{1}(t) &= y_{0} + \int_0^t f(s, \phi_{0}(s)) ds\\ &= 1 + \int_0^t \phi_{0}(s) ds\\ &= 1 + \int_0^t 1 ds\\ &= 1 + \sbracket{s}_0^t\\ &= 1 + t \end{align} \begin{align} \phi_{2}(t) &= y_{0} + \int_0^t f(s, \phi_{1}(s)) ds\\ &= 1 + \int_0^t \phi_{1}(s) ds\\ &= 1 + \int_0^t (1+s) ds\\ &= 1 + \sbracket{s + \frac{s^2}{2}}_0^t\\ &= 1 + t + \frac{t^2}{2} \end{align} \begin{align} \phi_{3}(t) &= y_{0} + \int_0^t f(s, \phi_{2}(s)) ds\\ &= 1 + \int_0^t \phi_{2}(s) ds\\ &= 1 + \int_0^t (1 + s + \frac{s^2}{2}) ds\\ &= 1 + \sbracket{s + \frac{s^2}{2} + \frac{s^3}{6}}_0^t\\ &= 1 + t + \frac{t^2}{2!} + \frac{t^3}{3!} \end{align} \begin{align} \phi_{n}(t) &= 1 + t + \frac{t^2}{2!} + \frac{t^3}{3!} + \cdots + \frac{t^n}{n!}\\ \phi_{n+1}(t) &= y_{0} + \int_0^t f(s, \phi_{n}(s)) ds\\ \phi_{n+1}(t) &= 1 + \int_0^t \phi_{n}(s) ds\\ \phi_{n+1}(t) &= 1 + t + \frac{t^2}{2!} + \frac{t^3}{3!} + \cdots + \frac{t^n}{n!} + \frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!} \end{align}

Thus the formula for $\phi_n$ is valid for every $n=0,1,2, \cdots$
