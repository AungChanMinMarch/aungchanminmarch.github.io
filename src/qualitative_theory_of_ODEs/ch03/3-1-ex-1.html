Ex 1: Determine an integral equation equivalent to the initial value problem $$y' = t^2 + y^4, y(0) = 1$$
$$y' = t^2 + y^4, y(0) = 1$$

Integrate both sides from $0$ to $t$ gives

\begin{align} \int_0^t y'(s) ds &= \int_0^t (s^2 + y^4(s)) ds \\ \sbracket{y(s)}_{0}^{t} &= \int_0^t s^2 ds + \int_0^t y^4(s) ds \\ y(t) - y(0) &= \sbracket{\frac{s^3}{3}}_0^t + \int_0^t y^4(s) ds \\ y(t) - 1 &= \frac{t^3}{3} + \int_0^t y^4(s) ds \\ y(t) &= 1 + \frac{t^3}{3} + \int_0^t y^4(s) ds \end{align}
Ex 1: Prove that the initial value problem $$y' = t^2 + y^4, y(0) = 1$$ is equivalent to an integral equation $$y(t) = 1 + \frac{t^3}{3} + \int_0^t y^4(s) ds $$

Suppose that $\phi(t)$ is the solution of the given initial value problem.

$$\text{Then } \phi'(t) = t^2 + \phi^4(t), \phi(0) = 1$$

Integrate both sides from $0$ to $t$ gives

\begin{align} \int_0^t \phi'(s) ds &= \int_0^t (s^2 + \phi^4(s)) ds \\ \sbracket{\phi(s)}_{0}^{t} &= \int_0^t s^2 ds + \int_0^t \phi^4(s) ds \\ \phi(t) - \phi(0) &= \sbracket{\frac{s^3}{3}}_0^t + \int_0^t \phi^4(s) ds \\ \phi(t) - 1 &= \frac{t^3}{3} + \int_0^t \phi^4(s) ds \\ \phi(t) &= 1 + \frac{t^3}{3} + \int_0^t \phi^4(s) ds \end{align}

Therefore $\phi(t)$ is a solution of the given integral equation.

Now suppose that $\psi(t)$ is a solution of the given integral equation.

$$\text{Then } \psi(t) = 1 + \frac{t^3}{3} + \int_0^t \psi^4(s) ds $$

Differentiating both sides with respect to $t$

\begin{align} \psi'(t) &= 0 + t^2 + \int_0^t \partialwrt[t] \phi^4(s) ds + \psi^4(t) \frac{dt}{dt} - \psi^4(0) \frac{d0}{dt} \\ \psi'(t) &= t^2 + \psi^4(t) \\ \end{align}

Therefore $\psi(t)$ is the solution of the given initial value problem.

Hence they are equivalent.
