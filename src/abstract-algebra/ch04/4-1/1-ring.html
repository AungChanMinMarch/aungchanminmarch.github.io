A nonempty set $R$ is said to be a ring if in $R$ there are two operations $+$ and $-$ such that:

  1. $a, b \in R$ implies that $a + b \in R$.
  2. $a+b=b+aforab\in R.$
  3. $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$ for $a,b,c \in R$.
  4. There exists an element $0 \in R$ such that $a + 0 = a$ for every $a \in R$.
  5. Given $a \in R$, there exists a $b \in R$ such that $a + b = 0$. (We shall write $b$ as $-a$)
  6. $a,b \in R$ implies that $a \cdot b \in R$.
  7. $a \cdot (b \cdot c) = (a \cdot b) \cdot c$ for $a, b, c \in R$.
  8. $a \cdot (b+c)=a \cdot b + a \cdot c$ and $(b+c) \cdot a = b \cdot a + c \cdot a$ for $a, b, c \in R$.

Because of Axiom (g), the associative law of multiplication, the rings we defined are usually called associative rings. Nonassociative rings do exist. Whenever we use the word ring we shall always mean “associative ring.”

Ring with unit

If there exists an element $1$ in a ring $R$ such that $a \cdot 1 = 1 \cdot a = a$ for every $a \in R$, then we say that R is a ring with unit.

Remember that identity element is also called unit element.

Observe that $\{ 0 \}$ is a ring but it is not a ring with unit since $ 1 \notin \{ 0\}$.

Domain

A commutative ring $R$ is a domain if $a \cdot b = 0$ in $R$ implies that $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.

Commutative Ring

A commutative Ring is a ring that satisfies commutative law of multiplication $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$.

Integral Domain

A commutative ring R is an integral domain if $a \cdot b = 0$ in $R$ implies that $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.

Some authors write an integral domain contain a unit element.

Division ring

A ring $R$ with unit is said to be a division ring if for every $a \neq 0$ in $R$ there is an element $b \in R$ (usually written as $a^{-1}$) such that $a \cdot a^{-1}=a^{-1} \cdot a=1$.

Field

A ring $R$ is said to be a field if $R$ is a commutative division ring.

Summary

Every ring is an abelian group under $+$. That is, every ring satisfies 5 properties:

  1. closure property,
  2. associative property,
  3. commutative property,
  4. existence of identity and
  5. existence of inverse
However a ring does not need to satisfy all 5 of them under multiplication. The following table indicates which properties under multiplication are required for different types of rings.
