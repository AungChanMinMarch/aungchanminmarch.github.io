A graph $G$ is an ordered triple $(V(G), E(G), \psi_G)$ consisting of a nonempty set $V(G)$ of vertices, a set $E(G)$ of edges($E(G)$ must be disjoint from $V(G)$), and an incidence function $\psi_G$ that associates with each edge of $G$ an unordered pair of (not necessarily distinct) vertices of $G$.

If $e$ is an edge and $u$ and $u$ are vertices such that $\psi_{G}(e) = uv$ then $e$ is said to join $u$ and $v$; the vertices u and u are called the ends of $e$.

Example 1
\begin{align} G &= (V(G), E(G), psi_{G}) \\ V(G) &= \{v_{1}, v_{2}, v_{3}, v_{4}, v_{5}\}\\ E(G) &= \{e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}, e_{4}, e_{5}, e_{6}, e_{7}, e_{8}\} \\ \text{ and } \psi_{G} \text{ is defined by }\\ \psi_{G}(e_{1}) = v_{1}v_{2}, \psi_G (e_2 ) &= v_2 v_3, \psi_G (e_3) = v_3 v_3, \psi_G (e_4 ) = v_3 v_4\\ \psi_{G}(e_{5}) = v_{2}v_{4}, \psi_{G}(e_{6}) &= v_{4} v_{S}, \psi_{G}(e_{7}) = v_{2} v_{5}, \psi_{G}(e_{8}) = v_{2}v_5 \end{align}
