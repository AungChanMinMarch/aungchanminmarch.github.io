A tube in the form of a parabola held with its vertex downwards and axis vertical, is filled with different liquids of densities $\delta$ and $\delta'$. If the distances of the free surface of the liquids from the focus be $r$ and $r'$ respectively, show that the distance of their common surface from the focus is

$$\frac{r \delta -r' \delta'}{\delta - \delta'}$$

Let $S$ be the focus and $ZZ'$ be the directrix of the parabola.

Let $2x$ be the distance of directrix from the focus. Then $SS' = 2x$

Let $M$ be the lowest point of the parabola. Then $SM=S'M=x$

Let $C$ be the common surface. Let $A$ and $B$ be the free surfaces. $SA=r$ and $SB=r'$

Let $AC$ and $BMC$ contain the liquid of density $\delta$ and $\delta'$ respectively.

$$\begin{align*} SA &= S'A' \\ SA &= SS' + SA' \\ r &= 2x + SA' \\ SA' &= r - 2x \\ \end{align*}$$ $$\begin{align*} SB &= S'B'\\ SB &= SS' + SB'\\ r' &= 2x + SB'\\ SB' &= r' -2x \\ \end{align*}$$ $$\begin{align*} SC &= S'C' \\ SC &= SS' - SC'\\ SC &= 2x - SC'\\ SC' &= 2x - SC \end{align*}$$

The pressures must be the same on the two sides of $M$.

$$\begin{align*} \delta'g MB' &= \delta' g MC' + \delta g C'A' \\ \delta' MB' &= \delta' MC' + \delta MA' \\ \delta' (SM + SB') &= \delta' (SM - SC') + \delta (SC'+SA') \\ \delta' (x + r' - 2x) &= \delta' (x - 2x + SC) + \delta (2x-SC+r-2x) \\ \delta' (r'- x) &= \delta' (-x + SC) + \delta (r-SC) \\ r'\delta'- x\delta' &= -x\delta' + SC \delta' + r\delta -SC\delta \\ r'\delta'- r\delta &=SC (\delta'- \delta) \\ %\delta' (x-r' \cos \beta) &= \delta' (x-SC \cos \theta) + \delta (2%x -SC - r\cos (\alpha)) \\ %\delta' (x-(2x-r')) &= \delta' (x-(2x-SC)) + \delta (2x -SC - (2x-r)) \\ %\delta' (-x+r') &= \delta' (-x + SC) + \delta ( -SC +r) \\ %-\delta'x+ \delta' r' &= -\delta' x + \delta SC - \delta SC + \delta r \\ %\delta' r' &= \delta' SC - \delta SC + \delta r \\ %\delta ' r' - \delta r &= (\delta' - \delta )SC\\ SC &= \frac{r'\delta' - r\delta }{\delta' - \delta} \\ SC &= \frac{r\delta - r\delta'}{\delta - \delta'} \\ \end{align*}$$
