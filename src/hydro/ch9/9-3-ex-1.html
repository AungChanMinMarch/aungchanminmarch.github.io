If $u=\frac{ax-by}{x^2 + y^2}, v = {{v}}, w=0$, investigate the nature of motion of the liquid. Also show that
  1. the velocity potential is $$-\cbracket{\frac{a}{2} \log (x^2+y^2) + b \arctan \tan^{-1} \frac{y}{x}}$$
  2. the pressure at any point $(x, y)$ is given by $$\frac{p}{\rho} = \text{constant} - \frac{1}{2} \frac{a^2 + b^2}{x^2+y^2}$$
Since $u=\frac{ax-by}{x^2 + y^2}, v = \frac{ay+bx}{x^2 + y^2}, w=0$, the velocity of the liquid is $$ \vec{q} = \frac{ax-by}{x^2 + y^2} \uvec{i} +\frac{ay+bx}{x^2 + y^2} \uvec{j} $$ \begin{align} \partialwrt[u]{x} &= \partialwrt{x} \frac{ax-by}{x^2 + y^2}\\ &= \frac{(x^2 + y^2)a - (ax-by)(2x)}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} \\ &= \frac{ay^2 - ax^2 + 2bxy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} \\ \partialwrt[v]{y} &= \partialwrt{y} \frac{ay+bx}{x^2 + y^2}\\ &= \frac{(x^2 + y^2)a - (ay+bx)(2y)}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\\ &= \frac{ax^2 - ay^2 - 2bxy}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\\ \text{div } \vec{q} &= \grad \cdot \vec{q} \\ &= \partialwrt[u]{x} + \partialwrt[v]{y} + \partialwrt[w]{z} \\ &= \frac{ay^2 - ax^2 + 2bxy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} + \frac{ax^2 - ay^2 - 2bxy}{(x^2+y^2)^2} + 0 \\ &= 0 \end{align}

Hence the equation of continuity is satisfied. Therefore the motion is possible. The vorticity vector is

\begin{align} \vec{\Omega} &= \curl \vec{q} \\ &= \grad \times \vec{q} \\ &= \curlExpand{u}{v}{w}\\ &= \bracket{\partialwrt[w]{y} - \partialwrt[v]{z}} \uvec{i}+ \bracket{\partialwrt[u]{z} - \partialwrt[w]{x}} \uvec{j}+ \bracket{\partialwrt[v]{x} - \partialwrt[u]{y}} \uvec{k} \\ &= (0-0) \uvec{i} + (0-0) \uvec{j} + \bracket{\partialwrt{x} {{u}} - \partialwrt{y} {{ v }}} \uvec{k} \\ &= \bracket{\frac{(x^2 + y^2)(-b) - (ax-by)2y}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}- \frac{(x^2+y^2)b - (ay+bx)2x}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}} \uvec{k} \\ &= \bracket{\frac{-b x^2 + b y^2 - 2axy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}- \frac{ -b x^2 + b y^2 - 2axy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}} \uvec{k} \\ &= \vec{0} \end{align}

The components of the vorticity vector are $\Omega_x =0, \Omega_y =0, \Omega_z = 0$. Therefore the motion is irrotational.
