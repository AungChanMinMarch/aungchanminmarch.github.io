--- title: "9.1B: Vorticity, Vorticity Components (or Components of Spin)" ---

Definition: Vorticity Vector

If $q$ is the velocity vector of a fluid particle, then the vector quantity $\Omega ( = \curl q)$, is called the vorticity vector or simply the vorticity and is a measure of the angular velocity of an infinitesimal element.

Vorticity Components

Let $\Omega = \Omega_{x} i + \Omega_{y} j + \Omega_{z} k$ so that $(\Omega_{x}, \Omega_{y} , \Omega_z)$ are the vorticity components or the components of the spin. Then, we have

\begin{align} \Omega &= \curl \vector{q} \\ &= \grad \times \vector{q} \\ &= \begin{array}{||ccc||} i & j & k \\ \dpartialwrt{x} & \dpartialwrt{y} & \dpartialwrt{z} \\ u & v & w \end{array} \\ &= \bracket{\partialwrt[w]{y} - \partialwrt[v]{z} } i + \bracket{\partialwrt[u]{z} - \partialwrt[w]{x} } j + \bracket{\partialwrt[v]{x} - \partialwrt[u]{y} } k \\ \Omega_x &= \bracket{\partialwrt[w]{y} - \partialwrt[v]{z} } \\ \Omega_y &= \bracket{\partialwrt[u]{z} - \partialwrt[w]{x} } \\ \Omega_z &=\bracket{\partialwrt[v]{x} - \partialwrt[u]{y} } \end{align}

If $\Omega_{x}, \Omega_{y}, \Omega_{z}$ are all zero, the motion is irrotational and the velocity function $\phi$ exists. If $\Omega_{x}, \Omega_{y}, \Omega_{z}$ are not all zero, the motion is rotational.

In the case of two-dimensional motion, we know that $\omega = 0$ and $u$ and $v$ are functions of $x$ and $y$ only and hence for two-dimensional case, \begin{align} \Omega_x &= 0\\ \Omega_y &= 0\\ \Omega_z &=\bracket{\partialwrt[v]{x} - \partialwrt[u]{y} } \end{align}

It follows that in owo-dimensional motion there can be at the most only one component of spin and its axis is perpendicolat to the plane of the motion
