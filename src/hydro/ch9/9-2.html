--- title: "9.2. Helmholtz's Vorticity Theorems. Properties of Vortex Tube." ---

{{title}}

(1) The product of the cross section and vorticity (or angular velocity) at any point on a vortex filament is constant along the filament and for all time when the body forces are conservative and the pressure is a single-valued function of density only.

Let $\Omega$ be the vorticity vector and let $\omega$ be the angular velocity vector.

Then we have $\Omega = \curl \vector{q}$ and $\Omega = 2 \omega$.

Let $\delta S_{1}, \delta S_2$ be two sections of a vortex tube and let $n_1, n_2$ be the unit normals to these sections drawn outwards from the fluid between them.

Again, suppose $\delta S$ be the curved surface of the vortex tube

Let $\Delta S$ be the total surface area of element and $\Delta V$ be the total volume which $\Delta S$ contains.

Then $\Delta S = \delta S_1 + \delta S + \delta S_2$.

By Gauss divergence theorem, we have $$\int_{\Delta S} n \cdot \Omega dS = \int_{\Delta V} \grad \cdot \vector{\Omega} dV$$

Since $\grad \cdot \Omega = \grad \cdot \curl\vector{q} = 0$, we get \begin{align} \int_{\Delta V} \grad \cdot \vector{\Omega} dV &= 0 \\ \int_{\Delta S} n \cdot \Omega dS &= 0 \\ \int_{\delta S_1} n_1 \cdot \Omega dS + \int_{\delta S} n \cdot \Omega dS + \int_{\delta S_2} n_2 \cdot \Omega dS & = 0 \end{align}

Since $\Omega$ is tangential to the curved surface of the vortex tube, $n \cdot \Omega = 0$ at each point of $\delta S$. Thus $\int_{\delta S} n \cdot \Omega dS = 0 $

\begin{align} \int_{\delta S_1} n_1 \cdot \Omega dS + \int_{\delta S_2} n_2 \cdot \Omega dS & = 0 \\ \int_{\delta S_1} n_1 \cdot \Omega dS &= - \int_{\delta S_2} n_2 \cdot \Omega dS \\ \int_{\delta S_1} \Omega \cdot dS &= \int_{\delta S_2} \Omega \cdot dS \tag{4} \\ \int_{\delta S_1} 2\omega \cdot dS &= \int_{\delta S_2} 2\omega \cdot dS \tag{5}\\ \end{align}

To the first order of approximation, (4) and (5) give

$$\Omega_1 \delta S_1 = \Omega_2 \delta S_2 \text{ and } \omega_1 \delta S_1 = \omega_2 \delta S_2$$

This shows that $\Omega \delta S$ or $2 \omega \delta S$ is constant over every section $\delta S$ of the vortex tube.

The product of the cross section and vorticity (or angular velocity) at any point on a vortex filament is constant along the filament. This value is known as the strength of the vortex tube.

A vortex tube whose strength is unity is called a unit vortex tube.
