Case I. When the filaments are in the same sense.

Let us consider two rectilinear vortices of strengths $\kappa_{1}$ and $\kappa_{2}$ at $A_{1} (z = z_{1})$ and $A_{2}(z = z_{2})$. Then complex potential due to stationary system is $$w = \frac{i \kappa_{1}}{2\pi} \log(z - z_{1}) + \frac{i \kappa_{2}}{2\pi} \log(z - z_{2}) \tag{1}$$

However, vortices situated at $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ would start moving due to the presence of each other. Let $u_1, v_1$ be components of the velocity $q_{1}$ of $A$, which is due to $A_{2}$ alone. Then, we have $$u_1 - i v_1 = \sbracket{\frac{1}{2\pi} \frac{i \kappa_{1}}{z - z_{1}} + \bracket{- \frac{dw}{dz}}}_{z=z_1} = - \frac{i \kappa_2}{2 \pi} \frac{1}{z_1 - z_2} \tag{2}$$ $$\therefore q_1 = \abs{u_1 - i v_1} = \frac{\kappa_2}{2 \pi \abs{z - z_2}} = \frac{\kappa_2}{2 \pi (A_1 A_2)} \tag{3}$$ $$\text{Similarly, } u_2 - i v_2 = - \frac{i \kappa_1}{2 \pi} \frac{1}{z_2 - z_1} \tag{4}$$ $$\text{ and } q_2 = \frac{\kappa_1}{2 \pi (A_1 A_2)} \tag{5}$$ From (2) and (4) $$ \frac{u_1 - i v_1}{\kappa_2} = - \frac{i}{2 \pi} \frac{1}{z_1 - z_2} = \frac{i}{2 \pi} \frac{1}{z_2 - z_1} = - \frac{u_2 - i v_2}{\kappa_1} $$ \begin{align} \frac{u_1 - i v_1}{\kappa_2} &= - \frac{u_2 - i v_2}{\kappa_1} \\ \kappa_1(u_1 - i v_1) &= - \kappa_2 (u_2 - i v_2) \\ \kappa_1(u_1 - i v_1) + \kappa_2 (u_2 - i v_2) &= 0 \\ (\kappa_1 u_1 + \kappa_2 u_2) - i (\kappa_1 v_1 + \kappa_2 v_2) &= 0 \\ \end{align} $$\text{Hence } \kappa_1 u_1 + \kappa_2 u_2 = 0 \text{ and } \kappa_1 v_1 + \kappa_2 v_2 = 0 \tag{6}$$

Since $\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{k_2} \neq 0$, (6) shows that a point G, the centroid of masses $\kappa_1, \kappa_{2}$ at $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ moving with velocities $(u_1, v_1), (u_2, v_2)$ is at rest. Hence the line $A_{1} A_{2}$ rotates about $G$. Since G is center of gravity of $\kappa_{1}$ and $\kappa_2$ we have

\begin{align} \kappa_{1} \cdot A_{1}G &= \kappa_{2} \cdot A_{2}G \\ \frac{A_{1}G}{\kappa_{2}} &= \frac{A_{2}G}{\kappa_{1}} \\ \end{align} $$ \frac{A_{1}G}{\kappa_{2}} = \frac{A_{2}G}{\kappa_{1}} = \frac{A_{1}G + A_{2}G}{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}} = \frac{A_{1} A_{2}}{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}} $$ $$\text{Hence } A_{1}G = \frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}} A_{1} A_{2} \text{ and } A_{2}G =\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}} A_{1} A_{2} \tag{7}$$ From Eq(3) \begin{align} q_1 &= \frac{\kappa_2}{2 \pi (A_1 A_2)} \\ &= \frac{\kappa_2}{2 \pi (A_1 A_2)} \cdot \frac{\kappa_1 + \kappa_2}{\kappa_1 + \kappa_2} \cdot \frac{A_1 A_2}{A_1 A_2} \\ &= \frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}} A_1 A_2 \cdot \frac{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}}{2 \pi (A_1 A_2)^2} \\ &= A_1G \cdot \omega \end{align} where $\omega = \dfrac{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}}{2 \pi (A_1 A_2)^2}$ and $A_1 G = \frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2}} A_1 A_2$.

Thus angular velocity of $A_{1}$ is $\omega$ about $G$. Similarly, we may show that the angular velocity of $A_{2}$ is $\omega$ about $G$. Hence the line $A_{1} A_{2}$ revolves about $G$ with uniform angular velocity $\omega$.

 Remark. As a particular case, let $\kappa_{1} = \kappa_{2} = \kappa$ and $A_{1} A_{2} = 2a$. Then, we have $$q_{1} = \frac{\kappa}{4 \pi a}, q_{2} = \frac{\kappa}{4\pi a} \text{ and } \omega = \frac{\kappa}{4 \pi a^2}$$ and the stream function is given by $$\psi= \frac{\kappa}{2 \pi} \log r_1 + \frac{\kappa}{2 \pi} \log r_2 = \frac{\kappa}{2 \pi} \log (r_1 r_2)$$ where $r_{1} = A_{1} P, r_{2} = A_{2} P$ and $P$ is any point in the fluid. The stream lines are given by $\psi = \text{const.}$ i.e. $r_{1}r_{2} = \text{const.}$, which are Cassini's ovals.
