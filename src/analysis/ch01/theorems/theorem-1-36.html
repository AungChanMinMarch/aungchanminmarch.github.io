--- title: "1.36 Definitions" ---

{{ title }}

For each positive integer $k$, let $\R^k$ be the set of all ordered k-tuples $$\vector{x} = (x_1, x_2, \cdots, x_n)$$ where $x_1,...,x_n$ are real numbers, called the coordinates of $\vector{x}$. The elements of $\R^k$ are called points, or vectors, especially when $k>1$. We shall denote vectors by boldfaced letters. If $\vector{y} = (y_1, ...,y_k)$ and if $\alpha$ is a real number, put \begin{align} \vector{x} + \vector{y} &= (x_1+y_1, ..., x_n + y_n )\\ \alpha \vector{x} &= (\alpha x_1, ..., \alpha x_n) \end{align} so that $\vector{x} + \vector{y} \in \R$ and $\alpha \vector{x} \in \R$. This defines addition of vectors, as well as multiplication of a vector by a real number (a scalar). These two operations satisfy the commutative, associative, and distributive laws (the proof is trivial, in view of the analogous laws for the real numbers) and make $\R^k$ into a vector space over the real field. The zero element of $\R^k$ (sometimes called the origin or the null vector) is the point $\vector{0}$, all of whose coordinates are $0$.

We also define the so-called "inner product" (or scalar product) of $\vector{x}$ and $\vector{y}$ by $$\vector{x} \scalarProduct \vector{y} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} x_i y_i$$ and the norm of $\vector{x}$ by $$ \norm{x} = (\vector{x} \scalarProduct \vector{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}= \bracket{\sum_{1}^{k} x_i^2}^{\frac{1}{2}}$$

The structure now defined (the vector space $\R^k$ with the above inner product and norm) is called euclidean k-space.
