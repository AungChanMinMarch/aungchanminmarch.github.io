--- title: "Theorem 9.3" ---

{{title}}

Suppose $X$ is a vector space, and $\dim X = n$.

  1. A set $E$ of $n$ vectors in $X$ spans $X$ if and only if $E$ is independent.
  2. $X$ has a basis, and every basis consists of $n$ vectors.
  3. If $1 \leq r \leq n$ and $\{ \bvec{y}_1, \dots, \bvec{y}_r \}$ is an independent set in $X$, then $X$ has a basis containing $\{ \bvec{y}_1, \dots, \bvec{y}_r \}$.

  1. Suppose $E = \{ \bvec{x}_1, \dots, \bvec{x}_n \}$.

    Since $\dim X = n$ the set $\{ \bvec{x}_1, \dots, \bvec{x}_n, \bvec{y} \}$ is dependent, for every $\bvec{y} \in X$.

    Suppose $E$ is dependent.

    Then one of its member can be removed without changing the span of $E$.

    Hence $E$ cannot span $X$, {% a 9.2 %}.

    Thus if $E$ is dependent, then $E$ cannot span $X$.

    Equivalently, if $E$ spans $X$, $E$ is independent.

    Suppose $E$ is independent.

    Then $\bvec{y}$ is in the span of $E$; hence $E$ spans $X$.

  2. Since $\dim X = n$, $X$ contains an independent set of $n$ vectors.

    part (a) shows that every independent set spans $X$. Hence every independent set of $n$ vectors is a basis of $X$.

  3. Let $\{ \bvec{x}_1 , \dots , \bvec{x}_n \}$ be a basis of $X$.

    Then $\{ \bvec{x}_1 , \dots , \bvec{x}_n \}$ is independent and spans $X$.

    Consider the set $S = \{ \bvec{y}_1 , \dots , \bvec{y}_r , \bvec{x}_1 , \dots , \bvec{x}_n \}$. Then $S$ spans $X$ and Since $\dim X= n$, $S$ is dependent.

    Put $\sum_{i=1}^{r} c_i \bvec{y}_i + \sum_{i=1}^n b_i \bvec{x}_i = \bvec{0}$, where $c_1, \dots, c_r, b_1, \dots, b_n$ are scalars.

    Since $S$ is dependent, there is at least one $b_k \neq 0$.

    \begin{align} \bvec{x}_k &= \frac{1}{b_k} \bracket{\sum_{i=1}^{r} c_i \bvec{y}_i + \sum_{i=1, i\neq k}^n b_i \bvec{x}_i} \\ \bvec{x}_k &\in \text{the span of } \{ \bvec{y}_1, \cdots, \bvec{y}_r, \bvec{x}_1 \dots, \bvec{x}_{k-1}, \bvec{x}_{k+1}, \dots, \bvec{x}_n \} \\ \bvec{x}_k &\in \text{the span of } S \setminus \{\bvec{x}_k\} \end{align}

    Clearly, $S \setminus \{\bvec{x}_k\}$ spans $X$ but is dependent.

    But repeating this process $r$ times leads to a basis of $X$ which contains $\{ y_1,...,y_r \}$.
