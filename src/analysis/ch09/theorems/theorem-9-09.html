--- title: "9.9 Matrices" --- Suppose $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ and $\{y_1, \dots, y_m\}$ are bases of vector spaces $X$ and $Y$ respectively. Then $A$ every $A \in L(X,Y)$ determines a set of numbers such that $$A x_j = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij} y_i (1 \leq 5 \leq n)$$ \begin{align} x \in X \Rightarrow x &= c_1 x_1 + c_2 x_2 + \dots + c_n x_n\\ Ax &= A(c_1 x_1 + c_2 x_2 + \dots + c_n x_n)\\ &= A (c_1 x_1) + A(c_2 x_2) + \dots + A(c_n x_n)\\ &= c_1 A x_1 + c_2 A x_2 + \dots + c_n A x_n \\ \text{Since } Ax_j \in Y, A x_j &= a_{1j} y_1 + a_{2j} y_2 + \dots + a_{mj} y_m \\ &= \sum_{i=1}^{m} a{ij} y_i (1 \leq j \leq n) \end{align} Hence we have \begin{align} A x_1 &= a_{11} y_1 + a_{21} y_2 + \dots + a_{m1} y_m \\ A x_2 &= a_{12} y_1 + a_{22} y_2 + \dots + a_{m2} y_m \\ \vdots \\ A x_n &= a_{1n} y_1 + a_{2n} y_2 + \dots + a_{mn} y_m \\ \end{align} so that $$ \begin{pmatrix} Ax_1 & Ax_2 & \dots & Ax_n \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} y_1 & y_2 & \dots & y_m \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \dots & a_{1n}\\ a_{21} & a_{22} & \dots & a_{2n}\\ \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & \dots & a_{mn}\\ \end{pmatrix} $$ $$\text{Let } [A] = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \dots & a_{1j} & \dots & a_{1n}\\ a_{21} & a_{22} & \dots & a_{2j} & \dots & a_{2n}\\ \vdots & & & & & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & \dots & a_{mj} & \dots & a_{mn}\\ \end{pmatrix}$$
  • The coordinates $a_{ij}$ of the vectors $A x_j$ (with respect to the basis $\{y_1, \dots, y_m\}$) appears in the $j^\text{th}$ column of $[A]$.
  • The vectors $A x_j$ are called the column vectors of $[A]$.
  • With this terminology, the range of $A$ is spanned by the column vectors of $[A]$.
Suppose $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ and $\{y_1, \dots, y_m\}$ are bases of vector spaces $X$ and $Y$ respectively. If $\vec{x} = \sum c_j x_j$ where $c_j$'s are scalors and $A \in L(X, Y)$, find the coordinates of $A \vec{x}$.
\begin{align} \text{If } \vec{x} &= \sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j, \text{ then } \\ A \vec{x} &= A (\sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j) \\ &= A(c_1 x_1 + c_2 x_2 + \dots + c_n x_n)\\ &= A (c_1 x_1) + A(c_2 x_2) + \dots + A(c_n x_n)\\ &= c_1 A x_1 + c_2 A x_2 + \dots + c_n A x_n \\ &= c_1 \sum_{i=1}^m a_{i1} y_i + c_2 \sum_{i=1}^m a_{i2} y_i + \dots + c_n \sum_{i=1}^m a_{i2} y_i \\ &\begin{array}{ll} =& a_{11} c_1 y_1 + a_{21} c_1 y_2 + \dots + a_{m1} c_1 y_m \\ & a_{12} c_2 y_1 + a_{22} c_2 y_2 + \dots + a_{m2} c_2 y_m \\ &\vdots \\ & a_{1n} c_n y_1 + a_{2n} c_n y_2 + \dots + a_{mn} c_n y_m \\ \end{array} \\ &= (\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{1j}c_j) y_1 + (\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{2j}c_j) y_2 + \dots + (\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{mj}c_j) y_m \\ &= \sum_{i=1}^{m} (\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}c_j) y_i \end{align}

The coordinates of $A\vec{x}$ are $(\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}c_j)$.
Suppose $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ and $\{y_1, \dots, y_m\}$ are bases of vector spaces $X$ and $Y$ respectively. Suppose next that an $m$ by $n$ matrix $A$, is given with real entries $a_{ij}$. If $A$ is defined by $A \vec{x} =\sum\limits_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} c_j} y_j$ where $\vec{x}=\sum c_j x_j$, show that $A \in L(X,Y)$

Let $\vec{x'} = \sum c'_j x_j$. Then

\begin{align} \vec{x} + \vec{x'} &= \sum c'_j x_j + \sum c'_j x_j \\ &= \sum (c_j x_j + c'_j x_j)\\ &= \sum (c_j + c'_j)x_j\\ \text{Since } A \vec{x} &= \sum_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} c_j} y_j \\ A (\vec{x} + \vec{x'}) &= \sum_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} (c_j + c_j')} y_j \\ &= \sum_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} c_j} y_j + \sum_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} c_j'} y_j \\ &= A \vec{x} + A \vec{x'} \end{align}

For any scalar $c$,

\begin{align} c\vec{x} &= c\sum c'_j x_j \\ &= \sum (c c_j)x_j\\ A (c\vec{x}) &= \sum_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} (cc_j)} y_j \\ &= c \sum_{i=1}^n \bracket{\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{ij} c_j} y_j\\ &= c A \vec{x} \end{align}

Hence $A \in L(X,Y)$.
